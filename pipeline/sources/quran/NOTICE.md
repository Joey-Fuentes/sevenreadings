# The Qur'an: parallels to the Bible, in Pickthall's translation

The Islamic reading in this app is the Qur'an itself: at a Bible passage
that the Qur'an retells or answers — Adam, Noah, Abraham, Joseph, Moses,
David, Solomon, Jonah, Zechariah, Mary, Jesus, the Law, the Judgement — the
Qur'an's passage is shown in full, in English. Each entry says in its
heading what the parallel is; where a pairing rests on Muslim exegesis
rather than on the text (the prophet foretold, the son sacrificed) the
heading says so.

Translation shipped: Marmaduke Pickthall, The Meaning of the Glorious
Koran: An Explanatory Translation (Alfred A. Knopf, New York, 1930).
Public domain: a United States publication whose copyright was not
renewed (Project Gutenberg cleared it for the United States in 2005, eBook
#16955), and its translator died in 1936, so it is public domain in
life-plus-seventy jurisdictions since 2007. The text is read from that
Gutenberg eBook, which interleaves three translations verse by verse;
only Pickthall's lines are used, and neither of the other two
translations is shipped. The Gutenberg header and licence text are not
part of what ships; the underlying text carries no Gutenberg restriction.

The pairings themselves (`pipeline/data/quran_parallels.tsv`) are this
project's data, edited by hand and released under CC0. They are a first
selection, not a concordance: additions and corrections belong in that
file. Verse numbering is the standard (Kufic) numbering, as Pickthall
uses it.

Not shipped: any tafsir. Ibn Kathir's and the other classical
commentaries exist in English only under copyright; if an open text ever
appears, it would sit beside these passages as a second Islamic entry.
